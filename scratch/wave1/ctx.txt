WAVE 1 BRIEF (read it completely; it is shared by four tracks).

THE PROJECT. supdb (/home/user/supdb) is a read-optimized embedded key-multivalue store; bench/ is its suite against LMDB and RocksDB. The user's aim: beat both everywhere except disk space, and "every loss should be reversible": a cell a comparator wins is a workload the engine has no strategy for (CLAUDE.md, "The suite lives in bench/", third consequence). Read /home/user/supdb/CLAUDE.md and /home/user/supdb/bench/CLAUDE.md first: they are the rules, and the failure shapes in CLAUDE.md are real (each cost a gate cycle).

WHAT IS KNOWN (all measured on this host, a Firecracker guest with 4 vCPUs, under a 1 GiB cgroup memory bound at 10M keys; docs/engine.md "Out of core" has the tables):
- Against LMDB with readahead off our point reads are 0.68x (54k vs 79-82k/s), faults per read 0.41-0.46 vs 0.27, because our file is bigger (hash region about 190 MB, touched by every read so resident, plus records about 1.3 GB) in a cache of about 0.94 GB.
- A standalone probe (bench/examples/zprobe, results in its prereg.md) shows records packed into LZ4 groups that each fill one 4 KiB page (68 B a key, 0.68 GB at 10M) take 0.000 faults a read once resident (262-280k reads/s) up to about 96% of the cache.
- The design that follows is /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-supdb/43547e2e-b745-59c0-b3a0-b32f7c7e7919/scratchpad/design/zpages.md (2,400 lines; a pre-registered, adversarially reviewed design; its section 8 is the staged plan, section 7 the registered predictions). Each track below is one stage of it, or a lead beside it. Read the sections your track names, not the whole file.
- The default policy dies under a 1 GiB bound at 3M keys: its anonymous memory reaches 760-820 MB at the lag sweep's full-rewrite point where the arm with no block cache holds 225-255 MB, and the arm whose cache is budgeted at 256 MB reaches the same peak: the budget counts the writer's tables (255 MB there) and something else holds the rest. `bench run` prints a `lagmem` line per lag point under SUPDB_LAG_COUNTERS=1 with the anonymous bytes, the allocator's in-use and free bytes and the writer's counted bytes.

HOW YOU WORK.
- You run in your own git worktree of the repository (the tool made it); the main working tree at /home/user/supdb belongs to the parent: never write there, never switch its branch. Create a branch named as your track says and commit your work on it in small commits whose messages describe the change (no model or tool names anywhere in code, docs or messages). Never push, never open a pull request.
- Build with shared target directories so nothing is rebuilt from scratch (bench/target is 15 GB; the disk has 10 GB free; a private target dir would fill it and RocksDB takes 20 minutes to build): export CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/home/user/supdb/target for the engine crate (cd to your worktree root) and CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/home/user/supdb/bench/target for the bench crate (cd to your worktree's bench/). Cargo will serialise builds on the lock; that is expected. Give tests a private TMPDIR (mkdir -p /tmp/wave1-<track>-tmp) and remove it (and every supdb-next-* store) when done.
- Before you hand over: sh scripts/fmt.sh and (cd bench && cargo fmt --check) clean; cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings clean in the root and in bench/; the tests you wrote and the existing tests that touch what you changed pass under cargo test --profile checked (root) and cargo test --profile checked (bench); the wasm build still links (sh web/build.sh) if you touched anything the wasm target compiles. Do not run scripts/check.sh as a whole: the parent runs the gate.
- The machine is shared with the parent's timing runs. Do NOT run bench run, bench ab or any throughput measurement unless your track says so. Counting things (sizes, faults, anonymous memory) is fine.
- Pre-registration discipline: a change comes with CELL, DECOMPOSITION, CHANGE, PREDICTIONS, VERDICT, STOP RULE, DONE WHEN (see bench/CLAUDE.md); write yours into the report, with predictions that a quantity the suite or your tests print can refute. Old shapes stay as arms (an Options flag and supdb-<name> / supdb-ingest<name>, mirrored at every site in bench/src/engines.rs); an arm differs from its default in the option alone, and a test says so.
- Report truthfully: what you ran and its output, what you did not run, what you could not verify. If the design is wrong about the code, say so with file:line and do the right thing.
