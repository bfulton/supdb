8. How to wait, which cost G1-5 a whole repair round. An agent never ends its turn to wait for anything: a turn that ends is taken as the step's answer, at once, with whatever is unfinished reported as unfinished. A command that outlasts one call (a full test run, the cost count, a mutant runner, a gate) is started in the background with its output and its exit status in a log ("...; echo EXIT $? >> log"), and waited for in a foreground call: `until grep -q '^EXIT' <log>; do sleep 20; done; tail -5 <log>` with the call's timeout set to 600000, issued again each time it times out, until the line is there. A call that begins with a bare `sleep` is refused; the Monitor tool and a background wait both return at once and leave nothing to do but end the turn, so neither is the way to wait, whatever another instruction of this launch says about Monitor. Between waits there is always something to read: the next mutant's diff, the listing, the notes to bring up to date. Before answering, kill what you started that still runs, put the tree back from your newest kept copy by sha256 if a mutant or a base is in it, and say in the notes what remains.
