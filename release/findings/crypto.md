# Encryption audit: supdb at C0 = b3a043dc

This is a read-only audit. It covers `supdb::crypt` (`src/crypt.rs`, `web/crypt.mjs`), `Encryption::Cipher` (`src/db/vault.rs`, `src/db/pager.rs`) and `Encryption::Kernel` (`src/db/dirkey.rs`), with their tests and examples. Nothing was compiled or run. The checkout's HEAD is `dd5dbbbc`; it differs from C0 only under `release/`, which `git diff --stat` confirmed.

**Summary.** I found no high-severity issue. I found no path where two plaintexts are sealed under one (key, nonce) in normal operation, no path where unverified plaintext is handed out, and no path where the store writes plaintext to its directory under `Cipher`. The four medium findings are at the edges:

- the finished-file envelope's nonce is made from only 8 of its 16 id bytes, and `encrypt_with_id` is public;
- an envelope is not bound to which object it is;
- `cipher_rewrap` is not a revocation;
- the README overstates what tampering is refused.

## What an attacker can substitute

An attacker who can write the directory but has no key can make the store open, without an error, as exactly the following.

**Under `Cipher`:**

- **Any historical prefix of the store's acknowledged history, back to the older of the two slots.** The attacker can:
  - cut any log file at a record boundary, by truncating it, changing a record, zeroing its first sector, or corrupting its wrapped key under `recycle_wal`;
  - turn a later chained boundary into a log end with the kind byte;
  - damage the newest slot, so the open falls back to the older one. That open is refused where the older slot needs a retired log (`needs_log`) or names a removed file; otherwise it opens, and envelopes only the newer slot named are swept.
- **Any earlier state whose files they kept**, by putting back a whole directory, or an older slot with its envelopes, from a copy.

No state outside the store's history is reachable. The tags and the sequence checks prevent it: a gap at the first applied frame ends the log or is refused; it is never skipped. `tests/cipher.rs` asserts that every outcome lies in the history.

**Under `crypt`:** any envelope under the reader's key, of any object.

**Under `Kernel`:** anything. Nothing is authenticated beyond the engine's CRCs, and XTS sectors can be replayed per position.

## Checked and sound

### Nonce uniqueness, file kind by file kind

- **WAL records.**
  - Key: a random 32-byte key per log file (`log_begin`, at every new file and every recycled life), wrapped under the store key with AAD `"log\0"||id`.
  - Nonce: 8 random bytes drawn when the `LogCipher` is made (at begin and at every resume, so a reopened torn tail never meets its old nonces), then a u32 count, with the prefix drawn again when the count wraps. The nonce is stored in the record.
  - Each seal takes a fresh nonce, the retry after a failed write's put-back included (`write_sealed` → `seal_into`).
  - Record AAD = (file id, offset, length): a record cannot be moved, repeated or cut.
- **Recycled log.** A new key per life. The last life's records are under the last key and fail their tags.
- **Segments.** A random token and key per segment (`persist_with`). The envelope id comes from `crypt::encrypt`, random per envelope. `Vault::rewrite` refuses to write a file that has an envelope, and `make` over an existing name gives a new token and key.
- **Ordered indexes and form files.** Under the segment key, each envelope with a fresh random id. Form files are written to a temp name and renamed, so a rebuild never reuses an id.
- **Manifest slots and key wraps.** The store key, with one `Nonces` per open shared by both. The AADs are domain-separated: `"manifest"||seq` (16 bytes), `"file"||token` (20 bytes), `"log\0"||id` (12 bytes), the marker head (40 bytes). No sealed blob can be presented as another kind.
- **Marker.** A random 96-bit nonce under the caller's key, at create and at each rewrap. An open only unwraps.
- **Checkpoints.** Refused under `Cipher` and `Kernel` (`Db::checkpoint`, `db.rs` l.50873-50889). `vault::copy` refuses paths under a cipher.
- **One-file store.** Envelopes are extents. Room is written over by new envelopes with new ids, never re-encrypted in place; moves copy ciphertext. Places are in the sealed manifest, with a first-page CRC as a hint.

### Envelope construction

- The chunk index is capped at 2^32 (`Header::new`) in both Rust and JS. GCM's internal 32-bit block counter is separate from the 96-bit IV.
- Chunks are at most 16 MiB and log records at most 4 GiB, both under GCM's per-message limit.
- `plain_len`, the shift, the id and the key id are in every chunk's AAD. The stored length must equal what the header implies. The zeroes between header and first chunk are checked in Rust (`checked_header`) and in JS. `Encrypted::open` verifies chunk 0, so `len()` is authenticated before it is used. JS answers `total` only after a chunk has verified.

### Verification before use

- Every decrypt copies the ciphertext and tag out of the (possibly shared and mutable) mapping first, then verifies the private copy: `crypt` `open_chunk` and `decrypt`, `pager` `Back::open_chunk`, `Stream`, WebCrypto. There is no double-fetch.
- A failed chunk is zeroed, not kept, and not lent; `read_at` zero-fills the whole destination. Form-file failures do not poison the store (`regenerable`, keyed on the engine's own label); every other failure poisons.

### No plaintext in the directory

- The decoy directory makes un-doored paths fail.
- `passes()` is default-deny: only `wal-`, `spare-` and `manifest*` reach the directory, and the engine seals each itself.
- `wal_set_aside` and the `dir-lost` mark are disabled under a cipher. Memfds are `MFD_CLOEXEC`.

### Key handling

- `Key` and `DirKey` zero themselves on drop, and the fscrypt add argument is zeroed after use.
- The `StoreCipher` (with the caller's master key) is taken out of `Options` at open and dropped (`db.rs` l.46278). `Debug` impls print `key_id` or `DirKey(..)` only.
- Randomness for keys, tokens and the store id comes from `getrandom(0)`.

### Kernel mode

- v2 policy, AES-256-XTS for contents, AES-256-CTS for names, `PAD_32`.
- `create` sets the policy on an empty directory before writing anything. `open` requires the exact policy. A key given to a plain directory is refused, and a locked directory with no key is refused by name.

### Test ciphers

- The `Toy` ciphers exist only under `cfg(test)`.

## For an outside cryptographic review

1. **The 64-bit random nonce prefix.** Are its bounds acceptable for the deployment? They apply to finished-file keys used for more than about 90k objects, and to the store key over more than about 90k opens. Alternatives are per-object or per-open subkeys, or a nonce-misuse-resistant AEAD (AES-GCM-SIV) for finished files.
2. **Key commitment.** AES-GCM does not commit to its key. Where a reader picks among several keys by the unauthenticated `key_id` or id (both documented patterns), someone who knows one of the keys can craft a chunk that is valid under two keys. Decide whether a commitment (for example a MAC of the header under a derived key) is wanted.
3. **The read-path obligation.** "A key absent" and "bytes that did not read" look alike to the readers. Integrity therefore rests on every read path asking `Pager::check` at its end, and on form-file failures falling back to the raw form rather than answering "absent". This is a convention, not a type. I did not audit every read path.
4. **Freshness.** The design concedes rollback and silent log truncation. Does the deployment need an external monotonic counter (the slot `seq`, or the log's high sequence, anchored outside the directory)?
5. **Side channels.** The pager's cache timing, and access patterns leaking through background work: form-file builds, conversions, hot-raw releases, merge timing. Also what the one-file layout shows across device snapshots.
6. **Fork and VM-snapshot safety** of the in-memory counter state in `Nonces`.
7. **Adapter correctness.** The `ring`, OpenSSL and WebCrypto adapters: in-place behaviour on failure, constant-time tag compare, the 32-byte key constructor.
