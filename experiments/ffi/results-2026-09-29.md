## What it says

**The C ABI costs nothing.** With keys from a table, the loop inside the
library and the C loop calling the ABI per read are level at both value
sizes (6/15 and 2/7, ratios within 5%). A Rust `cdylib` is a C library
with no boundary of its own to pay for.

**The copy is the cost, and it is a cache line's, not the memcpy's.** At
100 bytes, touching the value's first byte is free and touching its last
costs 62 ns (0/15): the record is inline, the lookup fetches the line the
key and the value's head share, and the value's tail lies on the next.
Copying costs 92-100 ns, the same line plus the copy. LMDB pays nothing
for either: the leaf page's lines are in cache by the time the search is
done. At 4 KiB the value is out of line and its first byte is a miss for
both engines (110 ns supdb, 260 ns LMDB), and the copy is 64 lines for
everyone.

**So the lead depends on what the caller does with the value.** supdb
over LMDB at 100 bytes: 2.2x handed a pointer, 1.7x having touched the
last byte, 1.6x having copied. At 4 KiB: 1.7x, 1.9x touched, 1.4x copied.
The suite's read is the first kind -- it counts the value's length and
touches nothing -- so its point-read figure is the pointer-only one, and
a caller who consumes the value sees the second or third.

**Python's tax is the call and the object, and it is nobody's language.**
The lean binding adds about 40 ns for the call, 45 more for a memoryview
and 75 more for bytes at 100 bytes; at 4 KiB the memoryview stays under
the borrowed C read plus the call while bytes pays the 64 lines. Through
Python, supdb over py-lmdb reads 2.0x with neither copying and 1.8x with
both copying at 100 bytes, 2.7x and 1.4x at 4 KiB. Every one of these
costs is on the caller's side of the boundary or in the lines the copy
touches; a C reimplementation of the engine would change none of them.
